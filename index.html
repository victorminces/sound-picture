See the shape and colour of a speech sound, as it happens.

The app asks the browser to switch off noise suppression, echo cancellation and automatic gain. This matters: all three are built to clean up speech on calls, and all three flatten the quiet high‑frequency hiss that /s/ is made of.

A vowel runs 20–30 dB hotter than the sibilant band, so without a tilt the vowels set the scale and /s/ sinks. The tilt is a fixed gain curve rising with frequency — the same offset every time, so productions stay comparable to each other and to a frozen outline.

Background noise

Hold it down for a second or two while nobody speaks. Whatever the microphone hears during that time — a fan, a hum, the room itself — is recorded and then subtracted from everything afterwards. Hold it longer for a better recording, and do it again whenever the room changes.
